How a project works, from first quote to final invoice

Contracting, MEP, joinery and development all run on projects. Switch between the two options and click each step to see what happens.

✔ Strengths

  • Budget vs actual for every project and task, in real time
  • Materials, labour, subcontractors and expenses all post to the project
  • Work-in-progress (WIP) and percentage-of-completion accounting
  • Invoice by milestone, by stage or by cost incurred
  • Timesheets with approval for staff and site teams
  • Simple for project managers and accountants to learn

⚠ Limitations

  • No standard retention or advance-payment recovery on progress invoices; needs a construction add-on or extension
  • No native BOQ (bill of quantities) or interim payment certificates; needs a construction ISV add-on
  • Project subcontractor valuations handled through purchase orders; BC's dedicated Subcontracting module (strengthened in 2026 release wave 2) is built for manufacturing outsourcing, not construction-style subcontract valuations
  • Resource scheduling is basic: capacity per resource, no skills matching
  • Revenue recognition via WIP methods, not a full contract revenue engine

Two parts working together

PROJECT OPERATIONS

The front office: quotes, contracts, project planning, resource booking, timesheets and expenses. Built for project managers.

PROJECT ACCOUNTING (in Finance)

The back office: project budgets and budget control, cost and revenue posting, WIP, revenue recognition, contract funding, billing rules and retention. Built for finance.

✔ Strengths

  • Contract billing rules: fixed price, time and materials, milestones, progress billing
  • Retention on contract invoices as standard
  • Contract funding: split a project between several funders or customers
  • Budget control that can block spending above budget
  • Revenue recognition for long contracts (percentage of completion)
  • Resource booking by role, skill and availability across companies

⚠ Limitations

  • Longer implementation and more training for site and project teams
  • Higher licence cost per user
  • Needs experienced internal admins to run it well
